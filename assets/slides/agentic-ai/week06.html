What people are doing · AI writes for readers

Summaries, callable papers, legible proofs

bioRxiv · since Nov 2023

  • AI summaries on preprints, generated from the full text by Sciencecast.
  • Three versions: a reader with little science training, a scientist in another field, a specialist.
  • The authors did not write them.
  • Currently paused? None on recent EEB preprints we checked.

Paper2Agent · Nature 2026

  • A paper and its code become an agent you can question or run on your own data.
  • "Rather than treating the original interpretation as fixed, the agent enables dynamic hypothesis re-assessment and, at scale, provides a systematic way to revisit conclusions across many studies."
  • Only works if the code runs and the methods are exact.

Mathematics · 2026

  • Nine mathematicians rewrote a model's disproof of the unit distance conjecture into 19 human-legible pages, then each signed a reflection.

Where it can enter · a starting list

Some ways genAI can enter the writing process

Before drafting

  1. Brainstorming, framing the question
  2. Literature search & synthesis
  3. Outline, structure of the argument

Drafting

  1. Prose from your notes or bullets
  2. Prose from artifacts: code → methods, tables → results, data → metadata
  3. End to end, from idea to paper

Revising

  1. Line editing, grammar, concision
  2. Translation, for writers working in a second language
  3. Critique: friendly reviewer, red team
  4. Consistency checks: text vs. code, numbers vs. tables, claims vs. citations

After

  1. Versions for other audiences: lay summary, talk, post
  2. Peer review: writing reviews, answering them
  3. Readers' side: summarizing, querying or running your paper

Small groups · ~20 minutes

Questions to think about

  1. Is there a "technical" / "creative" split in our writing? Does it change how you think about AI writing for those products?
  2. What is the objective of these different types of writing products (record, argue, think, reach, etc.)? What does a "good" product look like, and why do we care if it's "good"?
  3. Do you have any "hard lines" you draw re: AI writing?
  4. What guidelines would you propose to the EEB community?

Pick a note-taker and a reporter. You can record audio and have Claude summarize the transcript, if you want.
