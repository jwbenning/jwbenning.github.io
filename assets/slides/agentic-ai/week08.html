1.7 tone economy round-trip flight, New York – San Francisco (0.9 t counting CO₂ alone)

Per person vs. in aggregate

  • US data centers use about 6% of US electricity now, projected ~12% by 2030 (Lawrence Berkeley National Lab, 2026; range 9.5–15%). AI is an estimated 15–25% of that today and drives most of the growth.
  • It depends on the power source: the same use is ~0.5 t on the average US grid, ~0.1 t on wind and solar (building the hardware still emits), ~1.5 t on coal.
  • Water: small nationally; mostly at the power plants making the electricity, plus on-site cooling (~15%). The pressure is local: UT Austin projects data centers at 3–9% of Texas water use by 2040.
  • Use scales with access. Cornell says it will track the footprint of AI use across the university.

Method: Zeke Hausfather (climate scientist, Berkeley Earth), “The real energy use of agentic AI”, The Climate Brink, Aug 2026. US-grid carbon plus hardware manufacturing; Anthropic publishes no energy data, so the range is wide.

Tips to reduce

Re-reading the conversation: 97% of tokens, ~¾ of the energy

Every time the agent stops to decide its next step (~13 times per typed prompt), it re-reads the whole conversation so far.

  • Start a fresh session between tasks instead of continuing a long one.
  • Trim what loads into every session (instructions, notes, tool lists). A long CLAUDE.md is re-read on every call.
  • Give the full spec up front: one clear prompt with the goal and a definition of done beats five rounds of corrections, each of which re-reads everything before it.
  • Keep big outputs out of the conversation: have the agent write long logs and data dumps to a file and report a summary.
  • Use smaller models for routine subtasks: subagents are 21% of the energy, almost all on the largest model.

Discussion 2 · Research & academic integrity

Whose work is it, and how do we respect privacy?

Credit

  • Disclosure of use: what should be said, and where (methods, acknowledgments, a cover letter)?
  • Authorship: most journals agree AI cannot be an author. What about the person who only prompted? Or the proposal co-written with AI?
  • Scooping: will people stop presenting computational work in progress at conferences? Someone can photograph a slide and have an agent working on it before the session ends.
  • Accountability: an agent ran 20,000 calls unsupervised. How does the human remain accountable?

Confidentiality

  • Peer review: pasting a manuscript or proposal you are reviewing into a cloud model. Currently forbidden by NIH, NSF, and many journals.
  • Others' data: a collaborator's unpublished data, or anything shared with you in confidence.

Cornell's provost (26 Oct): a draft AI research integrity policy is now in place; details at ai.cornell.edu.

Discussion 3 · Unequal access

Frontier models are not free. How do we address fair access?

Cornell's 26 October announcement

  • ChatGPT Edu / Codex, Claude Enterprise (Cowork, Claude Code) and Gemini, under institution-wide agreements; data not used for training.
  • November: faculty, staff, PhD students. Early 2027: master's students and undergraduates.
  • “Base-level use” is free; faculty and staff can buy more capacity at a discount. Training required first.
  • The provost's own words: “access to AI tools at Cornell is uneven.”

Gaps that remain

  • Within Cornell: heavy agentic use outruns base tiers. Does extra capacity come from the PI's grant? Students in unfunded labs get the base tier.
  • Between institutions: a field station, a teaching college, a lab in the Global South: $20–200 a month per person, or nothing. Anthropic's Claude for Science seats are open worldwide, but only to PIs.
  • Language: best in English and other major languages; speakers of lower-resource languages get weaker results and pay more per word.
  • Can we learn from other attempts to equalize access? Open access and preprints around paywalls, Research4Life, publication-fee waivers, travel scholarships and hybrid meetings for researchers from under-resourced countries.
