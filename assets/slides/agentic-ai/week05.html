Exercise 2 · Claude's methods vs. the paper's

More detailed than the paper

More than the paper

  • Length: ~1,500 words (plain), ~2,200 (guarded), all on the statistics.
  • 23–40 statements true of the code that the paper's main text omits or words differently.
  • Transforms, spans, filters, constants: log(x + 1), loess span = 1, dropped stems, hard-coded residual SD.
  • Packages for each step, and robustness refits the paper puts in appendices.
  • Sometimes more correct: the paper says "log(fitness)"; the code, and all three runs, say log(fitness + 1).

Where it fell short

  • Missed small details: R² as a model check, Nagelkerke's pseudo-R².
  • Errors: 3 per plain run, mostly following the code's comments instead of what it runs (as the paper does). Guarded: 0, after its verifier fixed 19 points in its own draft.

Exercise 3 · proposal from a review

A Project Summary from a Week 4 review

Rehearsal topic: gene flow and adaptation at range edges. Prior work only from references the review cites.

Exercise 3 · what it wrote

Why does adaptation stop at a range edge?

  • The question: swamping by gene flow from central populations (Kirkpatrick & Barton 1997), or drift eroding the variance adaptation needs (Polechová & Barton 2015)? Or is the edge not a limit to adaptation at all?
  • Aim 1: replicated lab landscapes with an imposed gradient, crossing dispersal rate with deme size. Swamping predicts range contraction with dispersal at every deme size; drift limitation predicts an interaction.
  • Aim 2: at replicate field edges, transplant beyond each edge, measure the drift-threshold parameters, and carry crosses from climate-matched and central sources to F2 and backcross over three years.
  • Left for me: the study organism, number of edges, preliminary data, partners, trainees.
