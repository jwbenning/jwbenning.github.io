1. What is in the data

Between 1973 and 1992, Marks, Mohler, Gardescu and colleagues sampled forests, swamps, bogs, marshes and old fields across the central Finger Lakes region. The survey was the basis for the 2006 guide to the region's plant communities. At each site they laid out a tenth-hectare plot (50 × 20 m) and recorded three things:

  • Woody layer. Every tree, shrub and woody vine over 1 m tall, counted by species in three trunk-diameter classes (<1 cm, 1–5 cm, >5 cm at breast height). Basal area (the summed cross-section of the trunks) was calculated from those counts.
  • Ground layer. Plants under 1 m in 25 small quadrats along the plot's midline (10 at 20 sites): how many quadrats held each species, and its percent cover. Species seen in the plot but in no quadrat are listed too.
  • Site environment. Slope, aspect, landscape position, soil drainage, elevation and surface-soil pH (plot-level; for 111 of the plots).

Separately, 223 relevés (1985–1992) used a faster method, with visual cover estimates of the woody species only. They are kept in their own tables.

Everything is plain CSV, documented column by column in output/DATA_DICTIONARY.md. The 0.1 ha tables join on site_id. Species join to taxa.csv on (file family, code), never on the code alone. Across the whole survey there are 786 distinct names, and 744 ground-layer codes that are plants (the rest are logs, rock, water, moss and the like).

2. What was done to it

The raw files were never edited. A Python pipeline reads raw/ and writes output/. The whole thing rebuilds, and checks itself, with one command:

python3 run.py

Several datasets exist in two copies (an early-2000s export and a 2004–06 corrected workbook). The corrected copy is the base, because it carries documented fixes made from pencil notes on the 1985 printouts. The other copy, the surviving diameter tallies and the compilers' own summary sheets are then used to check it. Every judgement call is written up in DECISIONS.md with its evidence and the alternative not taken. Every affected value carries a flag ID pointing into flags.csv. The main problems the files posed:

Fixed-width text read by position

The 2002 tree file wraps every record over two lines, and values of 100 or more run into their neighbour (230.0022.23932 is two numbers). Parsed by character position, it matches the 2004 spreadsheet exactly on all 2,019 shared records, once you divide by 10 (it is per hectare).

An importance value that was never a number

The spreadsheet's IMPORTANCE column is the text file's importance value with a one-digit flag glued onto the end (33.90 and 1 became 33.901). Derived columns like this are not carried forward; they can be recomputed from the stem counts.

Two date systems

Some workbooks count days from 1900 (Windows), others from 1904 (Mac). Mixing them shifts dates by four years. Each workbook's own system is used, and every date is checked against the separate month/day columns and a sample of the scanned stand index.

Codes that mean different plants

COAM is Corylus americana in the ground-layer files but Cornus amomum in the tree files. ROSP is Rorippa or Rosa; SAPU is Sarracenia or Sambucus. Codes are therefore looked up per file family, and layers are joined on the cleaned name.

Blanks with three meanings

In the ground layer a blank can mean 'in the plot but in no quadrat' (documented, so filled with 0), 'cover not entered or a trace' (kept, flagged) or 'placeholder row' (dropped). In the tree file an all-zero row can mean 'no stems' or 'never entered' (site 87's trees exist on a separate tally sheet).

Independent copies disagree

Where an older copy, a diameter tally or the compilers' own summary sheet disagrees with the base file, the base value is kept and the disagreement is flagged: e.g. site 79 Betula nigra vs B. lenta, releve R37 white ash 10% vs 40%, site 207 basal area.

How the result was checked

The test suite (tests/test_outputs.py) re-reads the raw files independently of the pipeline and compares them with the outputs, row by row. It also checks the outputs against the compilers' own site totals (basal area, stem counts, cover matrices, the 10-quadrat sheet), and a dozen rows against the scanned stand index. It checks too that every column is documented and that every flag points to a real decision. The report is written only if all tests pass. To check the tests themselves, tests/mutation_check.py deliberately breaks five steps (wrong date system, naive text parsing, blanks left unfilled, one global species list, shifted diameter classes). When last run, the suite caught all five. Build environment: Python 3.9.6, pandas 2.3.3, xlrd 2.0.2.

3. What could not be resolved

Where the files do not settle a question, the value is left as recorded, flagged, and turned into a question for the people who collected the data (QUESTIONS.md). Nothing was guessed. These eight matter most for a resurvey:

questionflagged records
Q02Plot locations
No file in raw/ gives coordinates, a plot origin, an orientation of the 50 x 20 m plot, or the line the quadrats were placed on. Location is given only as a topographic quadrangle and quadrant, and many sites mark even that as uncertain. Relocating plots for the resurvey needs the field maps, datasheets, photos (several X sites say 'see photo'), or any corner markers. Where are they?		23
Q08Diameter classes and units, 1973-74 versus later
At sites 31-46 (1973-74), basal area of the small stems works out to exactly 1-inch-diameter stems. From 1975 on it works out to 2-cm classes. The column headers say '<1 cm, 1-5 cm, >5 cm'. Were the early plots tallied in inches (e.g. <1 in, 1-2 in, >2 in), and what were the exact class limits in each period? The resurvey needs matching limits to compare stem counts.		whole dataset
Q14Zero cover for species present in quadrats
Ground-layer records (count below) have the species in one or more quadrats but a cover sum of 0 or blank. The 2005 Notes say early stands 'have 0 entered for any woody spp in quads', but most of these rows are herbs, spread over every year, and most are single-quadrat occurrences. Were trace covers (<1%) entered as 0, or was cover not entered? Do the original datasheets have the values?		988
Q03Sites 187 and 188: 1991 or 1981?
Both are dated 30 June 1991 in the spreadsheet and the stand index, but every neighbouring site (184-189) was sampled 18 June to 2 July 1981. Is 1991 a typing error for 1981?		2
Q18Keys to the environment codes
No document gives the keys for 'aspect#' (1-5), slope 'position' (1-5), 'dist'/'disturb' (1-5) or drainage (1-6). Drainage is probably the six USDA classes, 1 = very poorly drained. Please confirm, and give the other keys. Flat sites have aspect# 3 in one sheet and blank in the other.		47
Q06Sites with no woody rows
Several sites (count below) have only an empty all-zero row in the woody file. For open sites (marshes, young fields) that may mean 'no stems >1 m tall'. But 47 and 49 are described as woods, and 87's trees are known to be missing from the files. For which sites was the woody layer sampled and truly empty, and for which was it not sampled or not entered?		19
Q10Undocumented species-code changes between file versions
Stem or cover values stay the same but the code changed, and no note explains it: site 79 BENI (Betula nigra, 2002) -> BELE (Betula lenta, 2004); site 208 ROSA -> ROSP; ground layer site 89 EUSE (Eupatorium sessilifolium, 2002) -> HEDI (Helianthus divaricatus, 2005). Which identification is right?		2
Q16Quadrat size
The methods say 1 x 1 m quadrats, but a sheet header says '1m2 (or 1/2)'. Which sites used half-square-metre quadrats? Also: why 10 rather than 25 quadrats at the 20 ten-quadrat sites (mostly old fields, plus site 140)?		whole dataset

The biggest practical gap is the first one. No file gives plot coordinates, orientation or corner markers, only a map quadrangle, which is uncertain for many sites. Relocating the plots will depend on field maps and photographs that are not in raw/.

Two more caveats for anyone comparing old and new data. First, in 149 of 180 plots, some species found in quadrats have zero cover (988 records). Frequency is still usable for those records, but cover is not. Second, the 1973–74 stem counts appear to use inch-based size classes. Stem counts by size class may therefore not be comparable across the two periods until Q08 is answered.

4. Using it

  • Start with output/DATA_DICTIONARY.md. Units are per plot (0.1 ha); multiply by 10 for per hectare.
  • Filter on flags before analysis. For example, drop or model the cover of ground-layer rows with cover_missing_or_trace, and exclude growth form 4 when counting species.
  • Do not compare woody records with ground-layer records on the code. Join through taxa.csv.
  • Names are as recorded, with spelling tidied. Nomenclature has not been updated to a modern checklist; that is a separate, reviewable step for the resurvey.
