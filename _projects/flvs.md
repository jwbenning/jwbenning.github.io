Around 1980, Peter Marks and Chuck Mohler surveyed the plant communities of the central Finger Lakes region in detail: roughly 210 quantitative tenth-hectare plots, 86 old-field plots, and ~250 relevés, all sampled by one team under one protocol across six counties. The survey became the empirical basis of *Guide to the Plant Communities of the Central Finger Lakes Region* (Marks, Gardescu & Hipps 1992), and then effectively disappeared: publicly the data has existed for four decades only as a classification into 18 vegetation types, and the plot-level records were thought lost. They resurfaced in a January 2026 office cleanout in Bradfield Hall.

Two things stand between those records and a resurvey. The data are undigitized line-printer output on backup discs, and the original site-location maps were discarded, so plot positions have to be reconstructed from on-sheet descriptors, historical aerial photography, and Peter's recall. We are working on both, and on what the 1980 baseline can be made to say on its own — a joint species distribution model over the historical plots currently attributes most among-plot community structure to measured environment, with a modest contribution from growth form and detectable phylogenetic signal.

Half a century is close to the ideal interval for detecting change driven by climate, land use, deer, and invasion, and comparable baselines for this region are rare.

*With Peter L. Marks (Cornell, emeritus).*
