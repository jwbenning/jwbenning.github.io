Expanding populations are expected to evolve higher dispersal, because the individuals at the front are, by construction, the ones that moved. That expectation comes from models of spread into uniform habitat. Real expansions run across environmental gradients, and a gradient changes the calculation: moving further also means landing somewhere you are less well adapted to.

We use simulation models, paired with the beetle experiments, to ask what happens when both processes operate at once. Environmental gradients oppose the evolution of increased dispersal during invasion, and, counterintuitively, *reduced* dispersal can make an expansion faster, by limiting the maladaptive gene flow that drags the front backwards.
